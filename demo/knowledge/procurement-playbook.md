# Procurement Playbook

## 1. Purpose

This playbook describes how the company buys goods and services, how suppliers are
approved, and how procurement data is recorded for reporting.

## 2. Supplier onboarding

- A supplier is registered only after a tax identifier and a signed master agreement
  are on file.
- Every supplier is assigned a category owner and a risk tier of low, medium, or high.
- High-risk suppliers are reviewed every six months by Procurement and Security.

## 3. Purchase thresholds

- Orders below 1000 USD may be placed directly by the requesting team.
- Orders from 1000 USD to 10000 USD require one written quote and a department head
  approval.
- Orders above 10000 USD require three written quotes.
- Orders above 50000 USD require a competitive tender.

## 4. Purchase orders

- A purchase order is created before any goods are received.
- Partial deliveries are recorded against the purchase order rather than a new order.
- Invoices without a matching purchase order are returned to the supplier.

## 5. Procurement reporting

- Procurement spend is reported monthly by supplier, category, and region.
- The procurement order table records the supplier, quantity, amount, and order date.
- Historical procurement rows are never edited; corrections are recorded as new rows
  with a reference to the original.

## 6. Records and retention

- Procurement contracts are retained for seven years.
- Quote comparisons are retained for three years.
- Restricted supplier banking details are stored only in the finance system.
